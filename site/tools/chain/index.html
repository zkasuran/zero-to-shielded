What the blockchain sees

The blockchain is a public record of payments. Anyone can look at it. On this page we call that person the Watcher. Here is what the Watcher can read when Maya pays her friend Sam. A note is a private message that can go with a shielded payment.

Try it

Flip the same payment

Scroll or use the switch. Watch what changes for the Watcher and what stays readable for Maya and Sam.

1

Transparent: everyone reads it

Maya pays Sam at his t1 address. A transparent payment is public, like Bitcoin. The Watcher sees who paid, who got paid and how much. A t1 payment cannot even carry a note.

2

Shielded: the Watcher sees noise

Now Maya pays Sam at his shielded address, the one that starts with u1. The amount, sender and receiver are encrypted on the blockchain. The Watcher cannot read them.

3

Sam still reads it

Maya adds a private note: "Thanks for lunch!" Only Maya and Sam can read it. Sam sees the 0.50 ZEC arrive in his wallet.

One exception to know

When older Orchard funds move to Ironwood, the newest shielded pool, the amount that moves is public. Who sends and who receives stays private. If Zodl asks you to migrate, pick Migrate with Privacy, because the amount that moves is public. A wallet you create today already uses Ironwood, so it never needs to migrate.

Sources: zodl.com, Zodl support: what is the turnstile, Zodl support: the Ironwood pool.
